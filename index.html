03 About

I come to code through design. Before I wrote my first line of CSS, I was already thinking in grids, hierarchy and color, and that background shapes how I build interfaces now.

I started in graphic design. I built posters, identities and campaign visuals, the kind of work where every choice has to earn its place on the page. Frontend development felt like a small step from that world, not a big leap. Same instincts, different tools. With Tech4Pride, I get to sit on both sides of the classroom. I coordinate the frontend track for other learners, and I show up as a student every week too. Explaining a concept to someone else is still the fastest way I know to actually understand it myself.

I'm not chasing flashy for its own sake. I want to build things that hold up: interfaces people can actually use, sites that load fast and don't confuse anyone, work that still makes sense six months later. If a project needs someone who can think about the design and the code at the same time, that's the work I want to keep doing.
